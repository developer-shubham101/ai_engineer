SECTION 2 — FOUNDER & LEADERSHIP
2.1 Founder: Dr. Vikram Nair
Dr. Vikram Nair (born 9 September 1961, Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala) is the founder and Non-Executive Director of Trishul Dynamics Ltd. A scientist by training and a builder by conviction, Dr. Nair spent eighteen years at the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) before leaving in 1997 to establish what would become one of India's most consequential private defence and technology companies.
Dr. Nair holds a B.Tech in Aerospace Engineering from IIT Bombay (1984), an M.S. in Propulsion Systems from the University of Tokyo (1986), and a Ph.D. in Guided Munitions Technology from IIT Delhi (1991). During his DRDO career, he served as Project Director for three classified long-range missile programmes and received the DRDO Scientist of the Year Award in 1994 and 1996.
In 1998, leveraging seed funding from the Aryan Capital Group and a small cohort of former DRDO colleagues, Dr. Nair incorporated Trishul Dynamics Ltd. in Gurugram with a founding team of 47 engineers. His founding philosophy — that India must never again depend on foreign entities for its sovereign defence capabilities — has remained the animating principle of TDL's institutional culture.
Dr. Nair stepped down from the CEO role in 2019 to focus on long-term strategic research and continues to chair the company's Scientific Advisory Council (SAC). He was awarded the Padma Bhushan by the Government of India in 2021 for his contributions to indigenous defence technology.

SECTION 3 — COMPANY HISTORY & TIMELINE
3.1 Founding Narrative
In the aftermath of the Kargil conflict of 1999, the Indian defence establishment confronted a painful truth: the nation's armed forces remained dangerously dependent on imported weapons systems and foreign maintenance contracts. Dr. Vikram Nair had already foreseen this vulnerability. Registered on 14 March 1998 — one year before Kargil — Trishul Dynamics Ltd. was his answer.
The name "Trishul" — the trident of Lord Shiva — was chosen deliberately: three prongs representing three founding commitments: national security, scientific excellence, and ethical enterprise. The three-pronged spear would later be reflected in TDL's three foundational subsidiaries (TADS, TBG, TCH), with a fourth, TOS, added in 2021 to represent India's space ambitions.
Starting in a leased facility in Manesar, Haryana, with 47 engineers and a seed capital of ₹ 120 Crore from Aryan Capital Group, TDL's earliest work centred on propulsion systems for guided artillery shells — technology Dr. Nair had been instrumental in developing during his DRDO years. The company's first government contract, awarded in 2001, was a ₹ 340 Crore order for integrated fire control modules from the Indian Army's Artillery Directorate.

5.1 Recruitment & Joining Process
5.1.1 Talent Acquisition Philosophy
TDL hires for values first, capability second. All recruitment decisions are governed by the principles of merit, diversity, and fairness. The company does not discriminate on the basis of gender, religion, caste, disability, marital status, or sexual orientation.
5.1.2 Recruitment Channels
Campus Recruitment: Conducted annually at IITs, NITs, IIMs, AIIMS, BITS Pilani, and selected international institutions. Internship-to-PPO conversions are a primary pipeline for engineering and research roles.
Lateral Hiring: Through TDL's internal ATS (TALENTBRIDGE), approved third-party staffing partners, and employee referral programme (TDL-REFER).
Strategic Leadership Hiring: CXO and VP-level positions are managed by Group CHRO with Board Compensation Committee approval.
Defence & Research Specialists: Retired armed forces officers and DRDO/ISRO scientists are recruited under a dedicated Specialist Track with relaxed age criteria.

5.5 Termination Policy
CONFIDENTIAL — HR LEADERSHIP & LEGAL ONLY
5.5.1 Grounds for Termination
An employee's service may be terminated by the Company on any of the following grounds:
Performance Termination: Failure to meet a documented Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) within the stipulated timeline.
Disciplinary Termination (With Cause): Substantiated misconduct including but not limited to: fraud, theft, bribery, gross insubordination, sexual harassment (as per POSH Act, 2013), data theft, and breach of classified information protocols.
Redundancy / Organisational Restructuring: Role elimination as a result of restructuring; subject to applicable retrenchment compensation under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
Medical Incapacity: Inability to perform essential functions of the role after exhausting all reasonable accommodation provisions.

Exit clearance initiated; F&F settlement within 45 days.

5.6 Resignation & Relieving Policy
An employee wishing to resign must submit a formal resignation through the HRMS portal (MyTDL). Email resignation is accepted only where HRMS access is unavailable, and must be followed by HRMS submission within 48 hours.
5.6.1 Process
Employee submits resignation via MyTDL portal with effective date.
Manager acknowledges within 2 working days. Retention conversation may be initiated (optional).
HR confirms notice period, last working day, and exit formalities.
Knowledge Transfer (KT) plan must be completed within the notice period.
Exit interview (mandatory) — conducted by HRBP, online or in-person.
Asset return, system access revocation, and clearance from all departments obtained.
Relieving Letter and Experience Certificate issued within 30 working days of last day.
Full & Final Settlement (F&F) processed within 45 days of last working day.
5.6.2 Notice Period Buyout
Employees may request notice period buyout (NPB) if they need to leave before serving the full notice. NPB is at the Company's discretion and is not a right. NPB amount = (Basic + DA) × remaining notice days / 30. Buyout requests must be submitted through the HRBP.
5.6.3 Absconding Policy
An employee who fails to report for duty for 5 consecutive working days without approved leave or communication shall be treated as having abandoned service. A registered letter is sent to the last known address. If no response within 10 days, employment is terminated with cause. No relieving letter is issued in such cases, and F&F is settled after deducting notice-period shortfall.

Trishul Dynamics Ltd. is committed to the highest standards of research and scientific integrity. All research and development activities conducted under the TDL banner — whether in-house, in collaboration, or under government contract — must conform to this framework.

SECTION 15 — ETHICS & AI USAGE POLICIES
15.1 TDL Ethical Framework
Trishul Dynamics Ltd. operates in sectors — defence, biotechnology, space, and clean energy — where the stakes of unethical behaviour are not merely commercial but can be existential. Our Ethics Framework is not an addendum to our strategy; it is its foundation.
The TDL Ethics Board, a sub-committee of the Board of Directors, meets quarterly to review ethical risks, adjudicate escalated concerns, and update this framework. It includes two independent external members: one with a background in defence ethics, and one in biomedical ethics.
15.2 Sector-Specific Ethical Commitments
Defence & Aerospace (TADS)
TDL does not develop, supply, or assist in the development of any weapon system that would meet the definition of an autonomous lethal weapon system (LAWS) as defined under CCW protocol discussions at the UN. All weapons systems currently in production and development require human authorisation in the targeting and engagement loop.
TDL will not supply weapons or defence technologies to entities or governments subject to UN Security Council arms embargoes, regardless of commercial consideration.
Dual-use export control compliance is treated as a non-negotiable; the Legal and Compliance team maintains a standing export control committee that reviews all proposed international transactions involving TADS products.
Biotechnology & Genomics (TBG)
TDL does not conduct and will not fund germline editing experiments in human embryos intended for reproductive purposes. All human genome editing research is strictly limited to somatic gene therapy applications under CDSCO and ICMR guidelines.
TBG's Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) has independent authority to halt any research programme on ethical grounds without requiring business unit approval.
TDL does not collect, process, or sell genomic data of Indian citizens for non-research purposes. Population genomic research conducted under government contract is governed by data sharing agreements approved by the Ministry of Health.
Clean Energy (TCH)
TCH does not pursue projects that require involuntary displacement of communities. All land acquisitions for solar or hydrogen facilities require free, prior, and informed consent of affected communities in line with the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights.
Supply chain due diligence: TCH does not source polysilicon from suppliers implicated in forced labour practices, per TDL's Responsible Sourcing Policy.
15.3 Artificial Intelligence & Algorithmic Systems Policy
TDL deploys AI and machine learning systems across multiple business functions — from predictive maintenance at TCH to target recognition support at TADS and genomic variant classification at TBG. This section governs the responsible development, deployment, and governance of all such systems.
15.3.1 AI Principles
Human Oversight: No AI system deployed by TDL shall make fully autonomous decisions in contexts where those decisions affect human life, physical integrity, or legal rights. Human review and override capability is mandatory.
Explainability: AI systems used in consequential decisions (HR, finance, clinical, defence) must be capable of generating human-interpretable explanations for their outputs. Black-box models without interpretability are not permitted in these contexts.
Fairness & Non-discrimination: AI models must be tested for bias across gender, religion, caste, and geography before deployment in HR, credit, or civic applications. Bias audit reports are maintained by the Group CTO and reviewed annually.
Data Minimisation: AI systems are to be trained and operated on the minimum data necessary to achieve the intended function.
Accountability: Every AI system in production must have a named System Owner (a senior employee) accountable for its performance, safety, and compliance.
15.3.2 Prohibited AI Uses
The following applications of AI are expressly prohibited across all TDL entities:
Facial recognition or biometric surveillance of employees, customers, or the public for non-security-critical applications without express consent.
Generating or amplifying disinformation, propaganda, or synthetic media (deepfakes) in any context.
Automated profiling of individuals for purposes of political targeting, social scoring, or ideological classification.
Any AI application that violates applicable Indian law (including IT Act, DPDP Act), MoD guidelines, CDSCO regulations, or international humanitarian law.
15.3.3 AI Governance Structure
AI Ethics Board
Sub-committee of TDL Ethics Board; reviews AI incidents, sets policy, approves high-risk deployments; meets bi-monthly
System Owner
Senior employee accountable for each AI system in production; named in the AI System Registry
AI System Registry
Maintained by Group CTO; lists all production AI systems, their risk classification, training data sources, and audit history
Third-Party AI Tools
All third-party AI tools (including LLMs, ML APIs) used by employees must be approved by IT Security before use; data entered into unapproved AI tools may constitute a data breach
Incident Reporting
Any AI system behaviour that causes harm or operates unexpectedly must be reported to the AI Ethics Board within 24 hours; AI incidents are included in the annual sustainability report
15.3.4 Employee AI Usage Guidelines
TDL encourages thoughtful and productive use of AI tools to enhance productivity. The following guidelines apply to all employees:
Approved Tools Only: Use only AI tools on the TDL Approved Software Catalogue. Consumer AI services (e.g., general public chatbot services) may not be used to process any TDL confidential, client, or employee data.
Verification Responsibility: AI-generated content, analysis, or code used in official work products must be reviewed, verified, and validated by the human employee responsible for that output. 'The AI said so' is not an acceptable defence for an error.
Disclosure in Research: Any published research or regulatory submission that used AI tools in any material aspect of data analysis or writing must disclose this use in the methodology section.
No Automation of HR Decisions: Managers may not use AI tools to automate decisions regarding hiring, promotion, performance rating, or termination without explicit CHRO approval and bias audit.
